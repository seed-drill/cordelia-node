# Decision: a removal fails safe

**Date**: 2026-10-24
**Status**: Proposed. Not built. Revised three times after its reviews. Settled for build after the third read (25 October).
**Cited as**: "decision 2026-10-24 rule 7", "decision 2026-10-24 §8". The numbers do not change. A number that is not used stays unused.

Words used throughout:

- **The record of 2026-10-04** is [`2026-10-04-a-persons-devices.md`](2026-10-04-a-persons-devices.md). **The record of 2026-10-19** is [`2026-10-19-a-relay-out-of-reach.md`](2026-10-19-a-relay-out-of-reach.md). **The record of relays** is [`2026-10-17-a-devices-relays-and-connections.md`](2026-10-17-a-devices-relays-and-connections.md). **The messages record** is [`2026-10-09-messages-between-your-own-agents.md`](2026-10-09-messages-between-your-own-agents.md). **The shared record** is `2026-10-10-a-shared-channel-for-messages.md`, on the branch `docs/shared-converge`: settled, and not built.
- **This version** is the first that carries what is below. **The version before** is 0.2.0-alpha.16. **Main** is commit abcc079d: every file and line below is of main.
- **A change** is a statement and its change entry (the record of 2026-10-04, 4.1 and 4.6). **Change n** is a change whose statement is numbered n. Two changes can have one number.
- **Made after.** A change is made after each change on its chain. **Made apart:** neither is on the other's chain (the record of 2026-10-04, 4.5).
- **A head,** on a device or at a relay, is a change that it keeps and that no other change it keeps was made after.
- **Its removed keys.** The keys that a device knows to be removed: every key that any change it has seen and checked lists as removed, on any branch. They only grow.
- **Who holds a secret.** A change's secret is held by each device that the change lists, and by each key that a record of an addition under that change adds: the hand-over gives it the secret (the record of 2026-10-04, section 6). A shared channel's seed is held by every device of the person's while the person holds the channel (the shared record, property 19), and by each key that an anew record seals it to.
- **Counts this device:** lists it, or adds it by a record that counts (as a hand-over does at `cordelia accept`).
- **Clear.** A secret is clear on a device where none of its removed keys holds it, as far as the device knows. A change is clear where its secret is.
- **To seal** is to write an entry under a secret, or to hand a secret on: in a hand-over, or in an anew record. **To send** is to push what was sealed to a relay.
- **The change a device follows** is the one it has applied and seals under; on a closed device, the one it applied last. Every other head it keeps is **set aside**.
- **Closed:** a device that follows no clear change. It replaces "in a fork".
- **Lineage:** the part of a change entry that a relay can read (rule 13).
- **The person's rulings of 25 October** settled the three questions of the first form of this record, and a fourth, on the shared list (section 12).
- Examples say laptop, desktop, tablet, phone and work laptop, and relay A and relay B. For shared channels they say Alice and Bob for two people, as the shared record does.

## 0. What this is

**The ruling** (the project owner, 24 October): "if you remove a device it should no longer be able to access any new information from that point, no failure modes that get around that, everything else is niceties." So a removal must fail safe by how it is built, and not by a warning.

**The fault.** An independent review found it, and a throwaway test with real processes showed it.

- A person has three devices, a laptop, a desktop and a tablet, and two relays. The laptop and the tablet reach only relay A. The desktop reaches only relay B. All three are on change 2.
- The desktop removes the tablet, through relay B. That is change 3, made on the desktop.
- The laptop cannot read relay B. It runs `cordelia renew`, and makes a change 3 of its own, which still lists the tablet.
- The tablet applies the laptop's change 3, and holds its new secret.
- When the two sides meet, each device that sees both is in a fork. `cordelia settle` makes change 4, with the tablet removed, and the tablet then reads that it was removed.
- **Until `settle`,** the tablet holds the secret of one branch, and reads what is written under it. And the removal no longer spreads by itself.

**Why, in the code of the version before.**

1. **A relay keeps one change entry.** Shown a change entry at the number of the one it holds, or below it, a relay answers with the one it holds, and stores nothing (`relay::shown` and `would_take`, `cordelia-storage/src/relay.rs`, lines 969 to 1008). Relay A holds the laptop's change 3. It never takes the desktop's, from a device or from a relay it works with. A device that reaches only relay A is never told of the removal.
2. **A device that sees both stops, and spreads nothing.** It keeps both entries, and goes on showing each relay the one it had applied (the record of 2026-10-04, 4.5; `person::shown` and `judged`, `cordelia-api/src/person.rs`, lines 924 to 1058). The tablet, shown the desktop's change, is in a fork: it is not told that it was removed.
3. **The record of 2026-10-04 says so itself** (4.5, "Which devices see a fork"): the devices of the branch that the relays hold "may never be shown the other, and go on". Each of them seals under a secret that the tablet holds, for as long as that lasts. With two relays that pass entries to each other, and no device that reaches both, it lasts for good.

What the version before gets right: a device that has seen both seals nothing. What it gets wrong: a removal can stop at a relay, and a removal takes effect on such a device only by stopping it.

**What this record does.**

1. A device keeps every head it is shown, and every key that any of them removes. It seals only under a clear secret, and takes nothing that a removed key sends. Of what it took before it knew, it carries on nothing of the removed key's that reached it after the removal was made, or in a branch made apart from it, whichever device carried it, and nothing stored over that; a key that the removed key added after its removal is removed too (rules 1 to 7).
2. A device that follows a change which is not clear moves, with no phrase, to a clear change that counts it. Where there is none, it is closed: it seals nothing until a person joins the two with the phrase (rules 8 to 12).
3. Each change entry says its lineage, and when it was made, in the clear. A relay keeps every head by it, says so in its handshake, and hands each to every connection before it says that it holds the entry shown (rules 13 to 19).
4. Every change made from here lists as removed every key that its device knows to be removed (rules 20 to 22).
5. A removal makes each shared channel anew, for when shared channels are built (rules 23 to 26).
6. The window that physics leaves is stated, with its bound, and a test holds it (section 8).

## 1. The properties

Each is a promise to a person, and each has tests (section 7). Where a property has a limit, the limit is stated with it.

1. **No change on any branch lets a removed device open what is sealed after the sealing device knew of the removal.** That holds for a renewal, an addition, a removal, a settlement and a recovery. Every secret made after a removal is new and random. None is derived from a secret that a removed device holds. **Its limit,** by the person's ruling of 25 October: a removed device still reads each later statement, which says who the person's devices are, by key and label, and which device made the change. In R1 that is a known limit (section 10).
2. **A removal takes effect on a device as soon as that device reads it, with no person's act,** even where the device also holds a change made apart that lists the removed device. `cordelia settle` may still join the two histories. It is never what makes a removal work.
3. **It fails closed.** A device that holds no secret clear of every key it knows to be removed (for example, each side removed a different device) seals nothing new. It keeps new content on the device, and asks for `cordelia settle`.
4. **Shared channels too.** Removing a device cuts it off from new content in every shared channel that the person holds. A seed does not change at a statement, so a removed device would go on reading it. Making each channel anew at the removal is what cuts it off (rules 23 to 26). **Its limit:** what another person writes there is cut off once that person's device has read the offer, and stays cut off unless that person says, at a terminal, to go on in the old channel. The person confirmed this line on 25 October (rule 26).
5. **Nothing sent after a removal is applied.** This is the project owner's own ruling (24 October: "agreed, nothing sent after removal is applied"). What a removed device seals or sends after its removal is never taken by a device that knows of the removal: no version of a file, no message between the person's agents, no record of an addition and no act in the shared list. Of what a device took before it knew, it carries on nothing of the removed device's that reached it in a branch made apart from its removal, or after the removal was made (by rule 2's cut time: the time in a change entry, less ten minutes), whichever device carried it there, and nothing stored over either: those go from the folders into local history (rule 7; the cut by time is the person's ruling of 25 October). A key that the removed device added after that time is removed too (rule 6). **Its limits:** what a device sent before it knew is at the relays (property 6). A device whose clock is behind the maker's by more than the margin carries what reached it in that excess. Until every device has this version, a device of the version before carries the removed device's late writing into the removal's change, and a device of this version takes it there (section 6).
6. **The one limit that physics sets.** Until a device has read the removal, it can seal under a secret that the removed device holds: while it is off, or cut off from every relay that holds the removal. Section 8 states it as a window with its bound, and says what narrows it.

## 2. The rules

### What a device knows of removals

**Rule 1. A device keeps every head it is shown.** It keeps the change it follows, and up to seven heads set aside: eight in all (`MAX_CHANGE_HEADS`). A head set aside goes when the device applies a change that was made after it, or keeps one set aside that was. A change is kept only where the phrase that the device follows signed it, it is well formed, and its lineage matches its statement (rule 13), or it has none (it was made on the version before).

- Where a ninth would be kept, the device is closed. Only a phrase that makes nine branches at once reaches that.
- **Where:** the rows of `person_change_entries` (`latest` and `apart` become the change followed and the heads), in a step of the schema; `person::shown` (`person.rs` 924).

**Rule 2. A device keeps its removed keys.** Every key that any change it has seen lists as removed is kept, whether the device follows that change, sets it aside, or finds it behind, and so is every key that rule 6 removes. A key is never taken out. At most 2,048 (`MAX_KNOWN_REMOVED`); past it the device is closed.

- **Its cut time,** for each generation the device was in that a change removing the key was made after: the time (rule 13) of the change on that change's chain that first removes the key, where the device has seen it and the change before it; otherwise the time of the newest change on that chain that the device has seen and that does not remove the key. Less the margin (`REMOVAL_TAKEN_MARGIN_SECS`). Of several, the earliest. Where no such change has a time (each was made on the version before), there is none, and rule 7 cuts everything of the key's in that generation.
- So the device keeps the time of each change it applies or keeps, after the change goes.
- **Why not the time of the first removing change it sees.** A device keeps heads only, and so does a relay. A device that hears only of the desktop's change 4 never sees its change 3, and would cut from change 4's time, carrying what the tablet wrote between. The tablet reads every later statement, so it would know when that gap is open. The time of an earlier change is never later than the removal, but for clocks.

- **Where:** a table beside `person_change_entries`. `Counting` (`person.rs` 243) takes them as its removed keys, in place of those of the statement applied alone (`Counting::of`, line 262; `publish::Standing::of`, `publish.rs` 408).

**Rule 3. Who holds a secret, as a device knows it.** A change's secret is held by the devices it lists, and by each key that a record of an addition under it adds, of the records this device has seen. A shared channel's seed is held as the words above say. A secret is clear where none of the device's removed keys is among them.

- **The holders are kept apart from the records.** The keys that records seen under a change add are kept with that change. They stay with it, with its mark past the bound below, for as long as the device keeps that change, followed or as a head set aside, and go when it goes. At a move, the key of each record under the change left is kept as its holder before the records go (rule 9): a change left because it was not clear stays not clear, so no move goes back to it. The bound of 256 on records that do not count (`keep_to_the_bound`, `person.rs` 565) drops a record, and never its key from here: a removed key's record counts for nothing (rule 6), and its key still holds the secret. At most 2,048 a change (`MAX_KNOWN_HOLDERS`); past that the change is not clear. Only a key that counts there writes a record that a device keeps (`see_addition`, 517), so no stranger can bring that about. **Any key that counts can,** a stolen device included before its removal is known: the change is then not clear, and the device moves or closes. It fails closed.
- **Holders that grow count as removed keys that grow.** A record arrives by the door (`take.rs` 236, `see_addition`), and changes neither the state nor the kept entry. A record that adds a removed key under the change followed fires rules 5 and 8 as a new removed key does.
- A record that this device has not seen is one more thing it has not read: section 8 counts it in the window.
- **Where:** one function beside `Counting`, which every rule below asks, over a table of holders beside the records.

### What a device seals under

**Rule 4. A device seals only under a clear secret.** That is every entry it writes in a channel of its own, every hand-over, and (when shared channels are built) every message and anew record in a shared channel.

- **Two places enforce it, and every seal passes one.**
  - `Standing::to_write` (`publish.rs` 422), which already refuses a device that does not stand applied, refuses a secret that is not clear. Every seal outside an apply takes its secret there: a publish (`publish.rs` 225, sealed at 509), a carry by command (`carry.rs` 225, sealed at 230), a name's word (`names.rs` 156 and 178, sealed at 136), the record of an addition and its hand-over (`adding.rs` 157, sealed at 300 and 372), a device's word that it left (`leaving.rs` 191, sealed at 209), the carried word (`at_relays.rs` 714, sealed at 784 and 844), a message (`sender.rs` 260, sealed at 565) and what an agent read (`marks.rs` 136, sealed at 208).
  - `come_to` (`person.rs` 1511), where every apply seals under the change's own secret, refuses a change that is not clear: the carry (`Carry::channel`, sealed at 1903) and the word that it applied (`write_applied`, sealed at 1948). Every apply passes it: a change shown (988), one made here (1151), a first change (1343), a recovery (1420), and `cordelia accept` (`adding.rs` 1094 and 1097).
  - **One seal is outside the two:** `write_over_dropped` (`adding.rs` 425, sealed at 452), a delete in a pair channel under the pair's own secret, which withdraws a hand-over. It hands on no secret. A closed device runs it too, so that a hand-over that carries a secret no longer clear is withdrawn (rule 5).
  - `applied_secret` (`person.rs` 1792) refuses nothing. Readers take the secret through it (`Standing::of`, `publish.rs` 408; the door, `take.rs` 203; `hold_name`, `person.rs` 768; `own_word`, `leaving.rs` 224; the look, `look.rs` 1117), and so do a move, for the secret it leaves (1546), and `leave()` (`adding.rs` 1251), which `cordelia phrase` and `cordelia init --new-key` run on a closed device.
- A device that leaves its phrase while it is closed (`cordelia phrase`, `cordelia init --new-key`) leaves without its word that it left: that word would be sealed under a secret that a removed key holds.

**Rule 5. From the moment a device knows that the secret it follows is not clear, it sends nothing more sealed under it, or carrying it,** on any connection: no entry, and no hand-over that waits in a pair channel. An entry that waits is taken out of what waits, and stays in the device's store. A hand-over that carries such a secret is dropped and written over (`write_over_dropped`, rule 4).

- It is today's rule for a change that a device can apply (the record of 2026-10-04, 4.2: "From the moment a device is answered with a change that it can apply, it sends nothing in an old channel"), made to hold for a removal on any branch.
- **The moment** is the commit of the transaction in which the device keeps the head that removes the key, or the record that adds it (rule 3), with its removed keys. That transaction commits at once. Only the apply, the move or the close waits for the sync cycle's turn. `as_a_change_where` (`state.rs` 1740) undoes its first doing where it comes to a change, and waits for the turn with nothing set, for as long as whatever holds the turn runs (a sync cycle, a history sweep, a restore: no constant bounds it). So what a change removes is not left to it. Each way a change arrives commits what it removes first:
  - **A change shown, or tried again** (`answered_as_a_change`, `device_entries.rs`): before the entry is judged, a transaction of its own keeps the keys that its change lists as removed, where the phrase signed it, and closes the device where the change it follows is then not clear (`person::know_what_is_shown`). That covers a change made after the one followed that removes a key, the most common removal: its apply then waits for the cycle with the device closed. It is skipped where the change removes this device, which rule 8 stops as removed and applies nothing.
  - **A change made apart:** the pass also holds the move back, so that rule 8 closes the device in the transaction that commits at once and moves it after (`person::with_moves_held_back`).
  - **`cordelia accept`** keeps what a hand-over's change removes, and closes a device whose change is then not clear, in a transaction of its own before it judges the hand-over (`adding::know_what_is_handed`), so a move to the hand-over's change, or to a head, waits for the cycle with the device closed.
  - **What is left is today's 4.2:** a change to apply that removes no key that holds the secret followed (a renewal, an addition) is kept only when its apply ends, and turns at other relays may send in the old channel until then. That secret is still clear, so it is outside this rule.
- **From that commit,** `Standing::to_write` refuses (rule 4); `Leave::has` (`leave.rs` 676) gives no leave; and the check once a stream is open (`open`, 828) asks what `has` asks of where the device stands, and not only the kept entry's ID (`keeps`, 1227). So a batch built before it is not written. **A push open at that commit and not yet answered is reset,** as `open` resets a stream at its opening (`leave.rs` 830): only what the relay already had whole can be stored.
- **At a wake,** a relay counts as having answered once its answer is judged, and not when it arrives (`show`, `leave.rs` 743). So the send pass does not start at another relay while a removal waits to be judged.
- **What was sealed before, and is sent with no secret.** `at_relays::channels` (`at_relays.rs` 320 to 333) sends, with no secret, what the device wrote outside its current channels. It sends nothing sealed under, or carrying, a secret that is not clear. `forget` (`leaving.rs` 258) keeps the device's word that it left only where the secret it was sealed under is clear.

**Rule 6. A device takes nothing that one of its removed keys signed,** from any channel, in any generation, from the moment it knows. A record of an addition for such a key counts for nothing. `cordelia add-device` refuses such a key. It is what makes property 5 true, the owner's ruling, for a device that knows.

- **Keys that a removed key added.** A record of an addition that a removed key signed counts for nothing where this device took it after that key's cut time (rule 2), or in a generation made apart from its removal. Today `Counting::counts` asks only whether the key itself is removed (`person.rs` 311), and `judge_again` (741) never takes a counted record back: so after its removal the tablet could add a key it controls under the laptop's change 3, and what that key wrote would reach the desktop's change. From the moment the device knows, it judges its counted records again by this. The key that such a record added is removed (rule 2), which fires rules 5 and 8 as any new removed key does, and so is each key that it added in turn: each holds a secret, and what each wrote is cut as the removed key's own (rule 7). A record taken before the cut time stays: a device added honestly before the removal stays the person's. (A recovery's look already leaves out keys that a gone device added: the record of 2026-10-04, section 9, step 3.)
- **Messages between the person's agents.** A message is shown only while its signer counts (the messages record, property 1). So from the moment a device knows of a removal, on any branch, `cordelia msg summary`, `msg read` and every hook show nothing that the removed key signed, and a reply to it is refused. What the device held of such a key's messages before it knew is shown by `cordelia msg log` alone, as the messages record has it for a removal today.
- **An act in the person's list of shared channels** that a removed key made counts for nothing where this device took it after that key's cut time, or in a generation made apart from its removal: the cut of rule 7, **decided by the person on 25 October.** An act in force from before stays, named with the command that undoes it, as the shared record has it (3.4, 3.5). This changes that record's property 9 (section 5, item 37).
- **One way in is a person's:** `cordelia sync carry --from <label>` brings in what a removed device wrote, at a terminal, by design (the record of 2026-10-04, 7.3). The "never" of this rule and of rule 7 leaves it.
- **Where:** `Counting::counts` and `Counting::judge` read the removed keys (rule 2). `adding.rs` 161 and 234 ask `statement.removes` today; they ask the removed keys.

**Rule 7. What a device took before it knew, and carries.**

- **What it sent is at the relays,** and the removed device can read it there. Nothing undoes that. It is the window of section 8.
- **What it holds,** it carries into the change it moves to or applies (rule 9; the record of 2026-10-04, 7.3), but for what the cut leaves out. While it is closed, it keeps it in its store, and carries it at the settlement.
- **What waited to be sent is never sent under the old secret** (rule 5).
- **The cut.** At any carry (a move, an apply, a settlement), the device judges each file by its **arrivals**, each with its key, the generation and the time this device stored it: every version of the file it stored that a key other than its own signed, with that key; and each link (`chain.rs`) of a version it stored, by the link's text hash and key, where that link stands in the version's chain more times than in any chain of the file this device stored before. A link is only its text hash and its signer, with no place or time (`entry.rs`), and stands again where a file comes to hold again what it held before: so a key that writes back a text it wrote before the removal makes an arrival, as does one that writes new text, and so does each copy carried of either. In a chain of 100 links, the most a chain keeps, where an earlier standing of the link may have fallen off the end, a removed key's link that is not counted more times than before is cut all the same: that fails safe. They are kept per file across carries, so a version carried on keeps when its links first arrived. For each removed key, an arrival of a version it signed, or of its link, is cut where it came:
  1. in a generation made apart from a change that removed the key; or
  2. in a generation that such a change was made after, after the key's cut time (rule 2), or at any time where there is none. **Decided by the person on 25 October.**
  The file goes back to the newest version stored before its first arrival cut. That arrival, and every version stored after it in that file, whoever signed it, are left out.
- **Why arrivals, and not who signed or what descends.** An arrival is when the removed key's text reached this device, whichever device carried it there and through however many carries: the laptop that took the tablet's late writing under change 2, and carried it into its own change 3 before it knew, leaves it out when it moves, and so does a device that took only the phone's carry of it. A line that a remaining device appends over it is stored after it, and goes too. So the cut reads no chain, which keeps 100 links and names versions the store has since replaced. It goes by when this device stored the entry, which the removed device cannot set.
- **At most 1,024 arrivals a file** (`MAX_FILE_ARRIVALS`), the oldest dropped first. The device's own versions make none. A busy file that other devices write fills them faster, since each version they write is one. A cut time before the oldest kept takes the file back to the newest version stored before that cut time, which fails safe.
- **In its place.** The slot carries the version the file goes back to. A folder whose file holds what is left out goes back to that version, as `cordelia restore` puts a version back. What is left out is kept in local history alone: not beside the file, where an agent would read it, and in no channel. The status says how many edits were set aside, and that `cordelia history` lists them and `cordelia restore` puts one back.
- **The put-back of an index line** (the record of 2026-09-30, 4.5) adds lines with no link, so arrivals do not catch them. On a device that knows, it takes no line from a version left out, or that a removed key signed.
- **Why the first goes by history, and not by time.** A generation made apart from a removal is history that the removal never saw. What the removed key wrote there came after the removal in history, though not always in time: a change made apart before the removal can hold honest work of the removed device, and that work goes to local history (section 9).
- **The limits of the second, stated:** a device whose clock is behind the maker's by more than the margin carries what reached it in that excess; one whose clock is ahead sets as much of the earlier writing aside, to local history. Where the device has not seen the change that first removed the key, it cuts from an earlier change, and sets aside what the key wrote between. Where no change on the chain has a time, it sets aside all the key wrote in that generation. Each fails safe, at the cost of honest writing that goes to local history. What the removed device wrote before the removal, and this device took after the cut time, stays behind, as 7.3 has it.
- In the fault, that is everything the tablet wrote under the laptop's change 3, and what reached the laptop under change 2 after the desktop's change 3 was made, less the margin: the laptop took it before it knew, and none of it reaches the desktop's change.
- From the moment the device knows, it takes nothing more of that key's (rule 6).
- **Where:** `Carry::channel` (`person.rs` 1833), whose `counts` and `carries` leave out what the cut leaves out; the arrivals, a table beside each file's record, written at the door (`take.rs`) and kept across a carry (`carried_entry`, 1883); the record of each folder moved at the carry (`move_records`, 1682), which names the version carried, with the file put back as `cordelia restore` puts it back (`cordelia-sync`, the record of 2026-09-30, 4.5b); and the put-back of index lines in `cordelia-sync`.

### Which change a device follows

**Rule 8. One function decides,** each time a device is shown a change that it does not keep, is handed one at `cordelia accept`, finds the holders of the secret it follows grown (rule 3), and at its first start on this version (rule 12). In this order:

1. A change that is the one followed, a head kept, or behind one of them: nothing is done, as today.
2. Otherwise the device checks it (the signature, the form, the lineage), adds its removed keys (rule 2), and drops each change set aside that it was made after.
3. **A change that lists this device's key as removed stops the device, as removed,** whether it was made after the change followed or apart from it. (Today a change made apart is a fork, even where it removes this device.)
4. A change made after the one followed, that counts this device and is clear: the device applies it, as today. It counts the device where it lists it, or where a record of the device's addition that counts comes with it (a hand-over at `cordelia accept`, 4.2 rule 3): a device dropped from a list and added again before it heard is added so. Where its secret does not open, the device stops, as today (4.5).
5. A change made after the one followed that neither counts this device nor removes it stops it, as in no list, as today (the record of 2026-10-04, 4.3), whether or not it is clear. One that counts it only by a record and is not clear is kept as step 6 says.
6. Anything else is kept as a head set aside. Where the change followed is still clear, nothing more is done: the device goes on (rule 11).
7. Where the change followed is not clear, the device moves (rule 9) to the best change it keeps that is clear, counts it, and whose secret opens for it: the one with the highest number, and of two at one number, the one whose hash is lower.
8. Where there is none, the device is closed (rule 10).

- A device that was removed, is in no list, or could not open a change takes no change, as today: the way on is a person's.
- **`cordelia accept`** judges a hand-over by this function, and refuses one whose change is not clear. Today `Brought::taken_by` (`adding.rs` 1139) judges with `statement::judge` alone: a branch that never heard of a removal could add the removed key again, renew, and hand that change to a device that knows, which would apply it.
- **Where:** `person::shown` and `judged` (`person.rs` 924 to 1058), `statement::judge` (`statement.rs` 667) and `Brought::taken_by`. `judge` keeps rules 1, 2, 5 and 6 of 4.2 for a change made after the one followed. `judged` gains the test of rule 3, the move and the closing.

**Rule 9. A move needs no phrase.** It is an apply, in one transaction, as 4.2 says: the device stores the change and its secret, leaves the generation it was in, carries what it holds (7.3, and rule 7's cut), and keeps the change it left as a head set aside.

- Two rules of 4.2 are not asked of a move: that the number is above the one followed (rule 2), and that the change was made after it (rule 6). Rule 5 of 4.2, that no removal is undone, is kept by the device's removed keys, which only grow.
- **A move to a lower number.** The renumbering of 2.3 runs forward only. A version, or a folder's record, whose revision is in a band above the change moved to is not carried as a version: the record is dropped, and its file meets the new channel as a new file does (4.2). A revision is in such a band only where a device jumped (2.3), so this is rare.
- **A command that waits after a change the device has moved from** ends with status 3, as for any change applied since (decision 2026-10-19, rule 8), and says: "a change made apart on <label> removed <label>, which this change lists: this device follows that change now".
- **Where:** `person::apply_judged` and `come_to` (`person.rs` 1454, 1511), and `Carry` (1808).

**Rule 10. A closed device seals nothing, sends nothing, and takes nothing in a channel of its own.** It is the state that a fork is today, and it is stored under the same name (`fork`).

- It keeps new content on the device: its folders keep their files, and nothing is published. What waits stays in its store.
- It shows each relay the heads that rule 18 says, and has no leave anywhere.
- It says, in red on its status line and in `cordelia devices`: "two changes were made apart, and none that lists this device is clear of a removed device: settle it with the phrase (`cordelia settle`)", with the lists of each head.
- It leaves the state by rule 8, when it is shown a clear change that counts it: the settlement, or a change made after it.
- `cordelia add-device` and the commands of `msg` refuse there, as in a fork today. `cordelia accept` takes there only a hand-over whose change is clear and counts this device (rule 8): it is how a closed device is added again. `cordelia remove-device`, `cordelia renew` and `cordelia settle` there make the settlement (rule 21).
- **Where:** `held_rows::set_state`, `at_relays::stands` (`at_relays.rs` 123), `device_entries::pass_at` (`device_entries.rs` 1006) and `say` (2921).

**Rule 11. A device that follows a clear change goes on, with any head set aside.** It syncs, sends and takes as any device does.

- `cordelia devices` names each head set aside, with its lists, and, where it is not clear, the removed key it lists: "a change made apart on laptop (change 3) lists tablet, which a change made on desktop removed: it is set aside". The status line is amber for seven days from when the head was set aside (`REMOVAL_NOT_APPLIED_SHOWN_DAYS`), and `cordelia devices` names it until a change joins it (rule 21).
- **Two devices that each follow a clear change made apart go on apart.** (Each removed the same device, say.) They do not sync with each other until a change joins them. Nothing is sealed where a removed key reads: that is a cost, not a fault.

**Rule 12. At its first start on this version,** a device that is in a fork applies rule 8 to the entry it kept apart: it moves, goes on, or stays closed. An entry of the version before has no lineage, and is judged by its statement alone.

### How a removal reaches every device

**Rule 13. A change entry says its lineage in the clear.** At the end of its content: a mark (the first 8 bytes of SHA-256 of the label `cordelia v2 lineage`, `LABEL_CHANGE_LINEAGE`), the time the change was made by its maker's clock (8 bytes, in seconds), the hash of its statement, a count, and the hash of each statement on its chain; then zeros. Each hash is the first 8 bytes of the statement's hash (`LINEAGE_HASH_BYTES`).

- **It fits, and the part for the devices does not move.** The part for the devices is fixed at 28,672 bytes (`CHANGE_ENTRY_DEVICES_PART_BYTES`). Today it is derived as 32 KB less the part for the phrase (`protocol.rs` 1080): left so, it would grow to 31,744 bytes when the part for the phrase shrinks, and every device of the version before would refuse every new entry as no change entry, with no leave at that relay. The part for the phrase shrinks from 4,096 bytes to 1,024: at every bound it needs 382 (a nonce, a tag, the secret, a count, and eight earlier secrets with their numbers). The lineage takes the 3,072 between: 2,074 at its bound, a chain of 256. Unit test 14 pins the three sizes in a published vector.
- The entry's two signatures cover all of its content, so only the phrase can write a lineage or its time. A maker's clock that is wrong is rule 7's limit.
- **A device refuses an entry whose lineage does not match its statement:** it is not well formed. An entry with no lineage (one made on the version before) is judged by its statement alone.
- **Why 8 bytes.** A relay uses them only to tell apart the statements of one phrase, and only the phrase makes one. Two of 256 share 8 bytes with a chance of about 2^-49.
- **Why the whole chain, and not the statement before.** A relay can then tell that one entry is behind another without having seen those between.
- **Where:** `cordelia-crypto/src/change_entry.rs` (its layout, building, opening, and an opener of the part for the phrase in each layout), and `protocol.rs` (`CHANGE_ENTRY_PHRASE_PART_BYTES`, `CHANGE_ENTRY_DEVICES_PART_BYTES`, `CHANGE_ENTRY_LINEAGE_BYTES`, `LINEAGE_HASH_BYTES`, `LABEL_CHANGE_LINEAGE`).

**Rule 14. A relay keeps every head.** In a slot, for one author, where entries carry a lineage, a relay keeps each entry that no other entry it keeps was made after, by the lineages: up to eight (`MAX_CHANGE_HEADS`), the highest numbers first.

- **An entry that another one it keeps was made after is not stored,** whether it is shown or passed. A change shown again is such an entry: whoever shows every change of a branch again pushes no head out.
- **An entry that was made after some that it keeps replaces them.**
- **An entry made apart from all that it keeps is kept beside them.**
- **An entry with no lineage** (made on the version before) is kept as today: while no entry in the slot has a higher revision. So a change made on the version before behaves as it does today, and a change made on this version is never dropped for one.
- **Where:** `cordelia-storage/src/relay.rs` (`would_take`, `shown`, `taken`: lines 969, 975, 537), and the relay's pull from a relay it works with (`relay_entries.rs`, `pulled`, 1284).

**Rule 15. A relay keeps room for a second head where it has the room, and never makes room.** It counts a slot whose entry carries a lineage at twice that entry's size, from when it takes the first such entry there. Once that is counted, a second head always has room, and a removed device that fills a relay with the old channels it holds cannot keep a removal out of it.

- **In a slot that is new,** the room for both is asked when the first entry comes, as for any new entry.
- **Where the first such entry replaces one of the version before** (a person's first change made on this version), the relay takes it, as it takes any replacement that is no larger, and counts the second share only where it has the room. Where it has none, a second head there is refused for room, as any new entry is, and each device that holds that head names the relay as lacking it for room (the record of 2026-10-19). That is a known limit, at a full relay, during the change-over.
- **A relay drops no channel to make room.** It cannot tell a change entry from any entry that ends with the mark, and anyone who holds a channel can write one. A way to make room would be a stranger's way to push out the relay's newest channels: the generations made just after removals, and the pair channels of devices being added.
- **A stranger's marked entries** are kept and passed as any person's (rules 14 and 17): up to eight heads a slot, each counted in the room of its channel and of the relay, and in the stranger's allowances. Those caps bound them, as they bound any entry.
- A third head, and those after it, take room as any new entry does.
- **Where:** the relay's count of its room (`Room`, `relay.rs` 201).

**Rule 16. A relay hands every head before it says that it holds the entry shown.** Shown an entry, whole or in short, a relay first takes it as rule 14 says, and then hands, one in each answer, each entry that it keeps in that slot and that it has neither handed whole on that connection nor been shown whole there, the lowest number first, and of two at one number the lower ID. It hands one whole (`ShowAnswer::Another`), or, to a short show, says its revision and ID (`ShowAnswer::Other`), after which the device shows whole and is handed it whole. **Only a whole hand counts as handed:** an `Other` hands nothing. Only once none is left does it answer that it holds the entry, took it, or refused it for room.

- **It takes first, and then hands.** A head that the entry shown was made after is then gone, and is not handed: a device of the version before is not handed a change behind its own, which it would judge `Behind`, and wait on.
- The answers are today's. A device of the version before reads each as it does now (section 6).
- A relay remembers, for a connection, each entry that it was shown whole or handed whole there, up to sixteen in a slot (the eight heads it can keep there, and the eight a device can keep: the change it follows and seven set aside), as it remembers today the one shown whole (2.4, item 5; `ShownWhole`, `relay_entries.rs` 243). So a device that shows each head it keeps (rule 18) shows each in short after the first time, and none that it was handed is put out and handed again. A ninth slot shown on one connection is not remembered, and its shows are handed the same lowest head each time: that costs only the device that shows it, and a device shows one slot, its person's.
- **The lowest number first:** a device that slept through two changes made apart meets the lower first, and so does not apply a higher one only to move from it.
- **Where:** `relay_entries.rs` (`shown`, `shown_short`, `ShownWhole`: lines 738, 822, 243) and `relay.rs` (`show`, `show_short`: 949, 1035).

**Rule 17. Relays pass every head on, until it is taken.** A head that a relay keeps beside another is passed on to the relays it works with, as an entry it took is today (`RelayEntries::took` and `pass_on`, `relay_entries.rs` 1139 and 1749, each 5 seconds: `ENTRY_OFFER_INTERVAL_SECS`). Today an entry is passed on only where it was stored as the newest of its author in its slot.

- **Every head the relay stores is passed on,** from a push, from a relay it works with, or from a show, whatever that show is answered: one answered "not now", since the asker is over its allowance, included. A later show of it is answered "held", and stores nothing.
- **An answer ends it, a refusal included.** A relay that answers for a head, with any answer, is not offered it again; one refused for room is left to the pull, as any entry is. A head that has its room counted is never refused for room (rule 15), so this is reached only at a full relay during the change-over, where offering again every 5 seconds would send 32 KB to a full relay at each offer and take nothing.
- **A head is offered again,** at each offer, to each relay it works with that has not answered for it, connected or not when the relay took it. Today `to_pass_on` (`relay_entries.rs` 1381) empties what waits even where no relay is connected, and an offer that fails is not made again: the relay is left to its pull, which reads 10,000 channels a pass, so at a large relay that is no bound at all.

**Rule 18. A device shows the change it follows first, and then each head set aside,** to each relay, on every pass: at a relay of the version before, only those whose number is not above it. So a removal stays at a relay for as long as any device is on that keeps it.

- **A relay of this version says so in its handshake:** it names the role `heads` (`RELAY_ROLE_HEADS`) among its roles. A node of the version before reads only "relay" and "bootnode" there (`p2p.rs` 2290 to 2295), and takes up to eight roles (`MAX_HANDSHAKE_ROLES`), so nothing else changes. Without it, a closed laptop that reaches relays A and B would never show the desktop's change 4 at relay A, and a work laptop that reaches only A would seal under a secret that the tablet holds for as long as that lasts (the second read, point 3).
- **At a relay that does not name the role, no head numbered above the change followed is shown.** A relay of the version before takes an entry of a higher revision in place of the one it holds (`would_take`, `relay.rs` 969). Shown the laptop's change 4 by a phone that follows the desktop's change 3, it would drop the removal, and the devices behind that use it would be handed change 4, and never told. A relay of this version gets such a head from the devices that follow it, and from the relays it works with (rule 17).
- **The change followed first,** for the same reason: a relay of the version before that holds change 2, shown first a head set aside at 3, would take that, and answer the change followed with it.
- A head set aside that a relay answers with another entry is not shown again in that round, and, at a relay that does not name the role `heads`, not on that connection. (A relay of the version before remembers one entry shown whole in a slot: shown two, each would cost 32 KB each way at every pass.) A relay that names the role remembers what it was shown whole (rule 16), so the head is shown in short at the next pass, and is taken there once the relay has room for it.
- **While the show of the change followed waits** after an answer that gave no leave (the wait of the record of 2026-10-04, §16, up to `OUTBOX_REFUSED_RETRY_MAX_SECS`), only that show is left out: each head is still shown, on every pass. Such a round gives no leave, and is no attempt at the relay for the status (the record of 2026-10-19, §1).
- **Leave comes from the answer to the change followed,** as today, and a later show of a head set aside does not take it away. **An answer that hands a head the device keeps gives no leave, and does not end the round:** the device shows the change followed again. **The same head handed twice in one round gives leave at a relay that does not name the role `heads`,** and ends the round: a relay of the version before does that, and it holds that head alone. **At a relay that names the role, it ends the round and gives no leave.** A relay of this version hands a head twice only once every head it keeps there has been handed on the connection, and it neither holds the entry shown, nor took it, nor refused it: by rule 14 it keeps what it will not take that entry beside (an entry made after it, or eight above it), and will not take it while it does. So it lacks the change, and the status says so.
- **A round** has room for 23 shows (`SHOWS_IN_A_PASS`, 6 today, becomes three times `MAX_CHANGE_HEADS` less one): a short show and a whole show for each of the eight heads a relay can hand, and a show of each of the seven heads the device sets aside.
- **Only the answer to the show of the change followed is the relay's latest answer** in the sense of the record of 2026-10-19 (§1). So a relay that holds a head set aside, and not the change followed, lacks the change, and the status says so.
- **Where:** `at_relays::to_show` (`at_relays.rs` 148), `device_entries::show_round` (`device_entries.rs` 1696 to 1821), which drops its early return on `shows.apart == Some(id)` (1793) so that an `Other` is always followed by a whole show, and `SHOWS_IN_A_PASS` (164), the leave (`leave.rs`), and the roles a relay names (`p2p.rs`).

**Rule 19. A change in what a device keeps wakes its pass at once.** Where it moves, closes, applies a change, or sets a new head aside, it shows at every relay it reaches at once, as a publish wakes the pass that sends today, and does not wait for its next pass.

### Changes made from here on

**Rule 20. Every change lists as removed every key that its device knows to be removed,** and lists none of them as a device. `cordelia remove-device`, `cordelia renew` and `cordelia settle` are handed the removed keys by the node, show them, and sign them; `cordelia recover` reads them itself (rule 22). The node refuses a change of its own that lacks one, or lists one as a device.

- **Its limit:** a statement lists at most 256 removed keys (`MAX_STATEMENT_REMOVED`), and a device may know up to 2,048 (`MAX_KNOWN_REMOVED`). A device that knows more than 256, or is past its bound, can make no change: `change_prepare` refuses before anything is asked, and says why. It fails closed. In R1 that is a known limit.
- **Where:** `commands::change_prepare` (`commands.rs` 1121), `change::prepare_change`, `prepare_settlement` and `prepare_recovery` (`change.rs` 125, 153, 192), and `person::apply_made` (`person.rs` 1203).

**Rule 21. A change made on a device that keeps a head set aside joins it.** There, `cordelia remove-device` and `cordelia renew` make a settlement: its chain holds the change followed and every head set aside, and its prompt asks of each device of a head set aside as `cordelia settle` asks today. `cordelia settle` is the same, with no device named. So any change made there joins the histories. On a closed device the three make the settlement too.

- `cordelia settle` on a device that keeps no head set aside is refused, as today.
- The part for the phrase of a settlement holds the secrets of every branch it joins, as many as fit (`MAX_EARLIER_SECRETS`), the newest first.
- **Where:** `change_prepare` (which refuses `settle` today on a device in no fork, `commands.rs` 1131), and `Statement::settle` (`statement.rs` 311), which comes to take more than two heads.

**Rule 22. A recovery lists one device,** as today. So its secret is clear by construction. It lists as removed every key that any change it read removes (the record of 2026-10-04, section 9, step 2, already settles two found apart). **It asks nothing of a device of the change it recovers from that another change it read removes:** that key is removed whatever is said, so the prompt says so in place of a question, and the look takes nothing that it wrote (property 5). The command asks, counts its room and prepares its statement from one list of every change read (`recover::Read`).

### Shared channels (for when they are built)

**Rule 23. A removal makes every shared channel that the person holds not clear.** A device seals nothing in a shared channel whose seed one of its removed keys holds. Every device of the person's holds every shared channel (the shared record, property 19), so a removed key held every seed the person held while it counted.

**Rule 24. The device that makes a removal makes each shared channel anew, in the same act.** After the transaction that applies the change, its node makes each channel anew as `cordelia shared anew` does (the shared record, section 7): a new seed, sealed to every key of the device's table of that channel but its removed keys. The person's own devices stay through the person's list.

- **The person's other devices follow,** with no yes: the filing is in the new personal channel, which the removed device cannot read (the shared record, 3.4).
- The removal's prompt names each shared channel, and says that it will be made anew without the device removed, and that the other people in it move with a yes of their own. The one yes of the removal covers it.
- A recovery does the same.

**Rule 25. A device that applies a removal and finds a shared channel that the person has not made anew** (the device that made the removal was lost first, or the filing has not come) sends nothing there, and says so: "<name> was held by a device that was removed: make it anew with `cordelia shared anew <name>`". It does not make the channel anew by itself: two devices would make two channels.

**Rule 26. Another person's device that reads an offer which seals to its person and leaves out a key sends nothing more in the old channel.** What its agents send there waits, and the send says why. It goes on when its person moves (`cordelia shared move`, with a yes, as the shared record has it), or says at a terminal, with a yes, to go on in the old channel, where the keys left out read: `cordelia shared stay <name>`. An offer that leaves this person out ("without you") changes nothing here.

- **Why.** A removed device of Alice's is a key to Bob, and Bob's device cannot know that it was removed. The offer is what it can read.
- **Decided by the person on 25 October: that is the line.** It changes a settled record (section 5, items 31 to 33). Its cost: any member can pause other people's sending by making a channel anew that leaves out a key. The pause is seen, and `cordelia shared stay` ends it at a terminal.

## 4. The candidate rule, judged

**The candidate:** a device never seals under a secret that a device removed on any branch it knows can open. In the fault, the laptop, on meeting relay B, drops its own change 3 and seals under the desktop's change 3, of which it is a member, with no phrase.

**The verdict: it is the right rule for sealing, and it is not enough by itself.**

- **It holds in every case below, for a device that knows of the removal,** with "can open" read as rule 3 reads it: listed, or added by a record that the device has seen. It is rule 4.
- **It fails where a device does not come to know.** A relay that holds a change made apart from a removal, at the same number or a higher one, never takes the removal, and never hands it (section 0). In the fault the laptop learns only because it comes to reach relay B. Let the two relays pass entries to each other, and let no device reach both: relay A never takes the desktop's change, the laptop never knows, and it seals under the tablet's secret for good, with the candidate rule kept to the letter. Rules 13 to 18 are needed beside it.
- **It says what not to do, and not what to do.** Rules 8 to 10 say it: move to a clear change that lists the device, or close.

**The cases.**

1. **The fault.** The laptop: its own change 3 lists the tablet, which it knows to be removed, so it is not clear; the desktop's change 3 is clear and lists it, so it moves (rule 9). The desktop follows a clear change, sets the laptop's aside, and goes on (rule 11). The tablet is removed (rule 8, step 3). No `settle` is needed. What the laptop sent under its own change 3 before it learned is at relay A, where the tablet reads it: the window of section 8. What it holds is carried into the desktop's change, but for what rule 7 cuts: what the tablet wrote under the laptop's change 3, what it wrote under change 2 after the desktop's change 3 was made, and anything written over either. Those are carried by nobody, and go from the laptop's folders into its local history.
2. **Each side removes a different device.** The laptop removes the phone, and the desktop removes the tablet. A device that has seen both: each change lists a device that the other removed, so neither is clear, and it is closed until settle (rule 10). The settlement removes both (rule 20) and is clear. The tablet and the phone are each removed by the change made apart (rule 8, step 3). The rule holds, and gives safety, not going on.
3. **A removal and an addition at once.** The desktop removes the tablet (change 3) while the laptop, on change 2, adds the phone. An addition is no change: the phone holds the secret of change 2, which the tablet holds anyway. The laptop applies change 3 when it reads it, since it was made after change 2. The phone is in no list of change 3, and stops, as today. The rule holds. Two variants:
   - The laptop renews after it adds the phone, and its change 3 lists the tablet and the phone. That is case 1. The phone follows the laptop's change 3, which is not clear, and no clear change counts it, so it is closed (rule 10). It is added again by hand: `cordelia accept` on a closed device takes a hand-over of a clear change that counts it (rule 10).
   - **A removed key added again by hand on a branch that never heard of the removal:** a person there adds the tablet again. Its record hands it that branch's secret. A device that has read the record and the removal takes that branch as not clear, whatever became of the record (rule 3). One that has read the removal and not yet the record seals under it until it reads the record: that is the window of section 8, by another road. `cordelia add-device` refuses the key on any device that knows of the removal (rule 6), and `cordelia accept` there refuses a change of that branch (rule 8).
4. **A recovery from an old state.** The relays lost the desktop's change 3, and the person recovers on a new machine from change 2. The recovery lists only the new machine, so it is clear by construction (rule 22). When the desktop's change 3 comes to light, each device judges by its removed keys. The new machine stays on its own, unless it has since added again by hand a key that change 3 removed, where it is closed. The desktop is removed where the person said it was gone. Otherwise it goes on, with the recovery set aside, and the two are joined by a change made on a device that has seen both (rule 21), whose prompt asks whether the desktop stays. The rule holds.
5. **A device that was off through several changes.** The phone, on change 2, wakes. Relay A holds two heads: the laptop's change 4 (after its own change 3, both listing the tablet) and the desktop's change 3. The relay hands change 3 first (rule 16): the phone applies it (made after change 2, clear, it lists the phone), and then sets change 4 aside. It shows relay A change 3, and not change 4, which is above it (rule 18). Where another relay, of the version before, hands it change 4 first, it applies that, and then moves to change 3 at a lower number (rule 9). It sends nothing under change 4 between the two: a device that wakes sends nothing until every relay has answered and the answer is judged (the record of 2026-10-04, 4.6; rule 5), and a relay of this version answers only after it has handed every head. The rule holds.
6. **A removed device that shows old changes again.** Each is behind a head that a relay of this version keeps, and is not stored there (rule 14), and is behind what a device keeps (rule 8, step 1). It pushes no head out. The rule holds.
7. **A lost maker's change that comes to light** (the record of 2026-10-04, 7.6). Relays keep both heads, and a device that is behind is handed both before it has leave. The change made again removes the lost maker, so the lost maker's change is not clear, and the device follows the other. The rule holds, and better than today, where a device far behind follows the lost maker's change until it is shown the other.

## 6. A device of the version before

**What a device of 0.2.0-alpha.16 meets.**

- **A change entry made on this version.** It opens the part for the devices, which has not moved and keeps its size (rule 13), and applies the change as any; its check of the zeros is inside that part. A change is made on a device of this version.
- **A relay of this version that keeps two heads.** It is handed the other with today's answer (`Another`), judges a fork as today, and stops. It fails closed. A device behind both is handed the lower first, applies it where it can, and is then handed the other, and stops. A relay of this version hands no change that is behind another it keeps, nor one behind the entry shown, which it takes first (rules 14 and 16).
- **A device of this version, and a relay of the version before.** That relay keeps one entry, as today. **Nothing a device does is less safe for it:** a device shows it the change it follows first, and no head numbered above it (rule 18), so it never takes a head set aside in place of a removal. Where it hands one head that the device keeps twice in a round, that gives leave (rule 18), so the device goes on syncing through it.
- **A change made on the version before** has no lineage and no time. A relay of this version keeps it as today (rule 14), and rule 7 cuts by its generations alone.
- **A device in a fork that takes this version** judges its two entries at its first start (rule 12).
- **The handshake** keeps its protocol version: each end offers, and accepts, only its own (`handshake.rs` 115, 116 and 224). A relay of this version adds the role `heads` to its roles, which a node of the version before passes over (rule 18).
- **A mixed fleet, a limit of the change-over.** At its apply of the removal, a device of the version before carries the removed device's late writing into the removal's change (the record of 2026-10-04, property 2), and a device of this version takes it there: it arrives in a clear generation, where no carry cuts it. **Can it ship without every device taking it at once? Yes.**

- **The order:** the relays first, where a person runs their own (the default relays are the project's), then the devices, one at a time and in any order. Nothing breaks where the order is not kept: a relay of the version before stores a change entry of this version, as it checks only the two signatures.

## 8. The window, for the threat model

**What physics leaves.** A device that has not read a removal does not know of it. Until it reads it, it seals under the secret of the change it follows, which the removed device may hold, and it sends what it seals to its relays. The removed device reads what was sent at a relay that both of them reach.

**Decided: the window is stated, 90 seconds at most, and a test holds it. It is not made shorter, but in one place.** The window is for a device that does not know yet, and it cannot be closed: for a device that is off, it has no bound at all (property 6). What a person needs is a number that is true, and a test that keeps it true. Each way to make it shorter costs more than the seconds it gains. Checking every relay before each send lets one slow relay hold up all the others, which the record of 2026-10-04 (16) chose against. Stopping every send already on its way adds a stop to every stream, for some 20 seconds. The one place that is changed is between relays, where today there is no bound at all for a relay that was not connected: a head is now offered again until it is taken (rule 17).

**Its bound,** from the moment that a relay holds the removal, by the constants of today:

- **A device connected to that relay** reads it within 80 seconds, by the constants: its leave there runs out within 10 seconds (`SHOW_LEAVE_SECS`); the request already on its way can take 30 (an open, a write and a read, each with the 10 seconds of `STREAM_TIMEOUT_SECS`); a pass passes over a relay whose turn is still running (`try_lock`, `device_entries.rs` 1311), so the show waits up to 10 more for the next pass; and the show's own stream takes up to 30. Meanwhile it can send at another relay that lacks the removal. Once it has read it, nothing more is written (it has read it at the commit that keeps what the change removes, before any apply or move waits for the sync cycle's turn, rule 5; that wait has no bound of its own, and is outside the window), and a push still open is reset (rule 5), but one whose last byte was already sent can still be stored within its 10 seconds. So 90 seconds at most: test 6 holds each stage near its limit, and holds the whole to 90.
- **A relay that works with one that holds it** comes to hold it within 5 seconds of being connected to it (`ENTRY_OFFER_INTERVAL_SECS`, rule 17), whether or not it was connected when the other took it.
- **A device that wakes** sends nothing until each relay it is set up with has answered and the answer is judged, or 30 seconds have gone (`WAKE_WAIT_SECS`). Where a relay that holds the removal answers within that, it sends nothing under the old secret.
- **A device that reaches no relay which holds the removal,** and whose relays get it from no device and no relay, seals and sends so for as long as that lasts. There is no bound: that is the limit.
- **A head numbered above the change a device follows is not shown at a relay of the version before** (rule 18). Where a closed device is the only one that could bring such a head to such a relay, it does not: the cost of never dropping a removal there, until the relay takes this version. At a relay of this version it shows every head.
- **A removed device at the same address as a device** can use up the address's limits at a relay, and delay that device's hearing there (the record of 2026-10-04, 2.5, "what is not defended"). That is one more way of being cut off.
- **A record of an addition that hands a removed key a secret,** on a branch that never heard of its removal, is one more thing a device may not yet have read (section 4, case 3).

**What narrows it,** each in a rule above:

- **Relays pass every head on, and offer it again until it is taken:** within 5 seconds of being connected (rule 17).
- **A device shows the heads it keeps on every pass,** so a removal is at every relay that a device which follows it reaches (rule 18).
- **A change in what a device keeps wakes its pass at once,** so it tells its relays without waiting for the next pass (rule 19).
- **A device sends nothing more from the moment it knows** (rule 5), and fetches before it sends: the leave of 10 seconds makes every send at a relay follow a show there by 10 seconds at most.

**For the threat model (T16).** In place of its sentence "(A device that has not heard of the removal still writes where the removed device can read, and still takes what the removed device writes there. ...)": "A device that has not read the removal still seals under a secret that the removed device holds, and still takes what the removed device writes there. It reads the removal within 90 seconds of any relay it is connected to holding it, and a relay holds it within 5 seconds of being connected to a relay that holds it. A device that reaches no relay which holds the removal writes so for as long as that lasts, and the removed device reads it at any relay that both reach. Once a device has read the removal, on any branch, it seals nothing that the removed device can open, and takes nothing that the removed device sends, messages between the person's agents included. Of what it took before it knew, it carries nothing of the removed device's that reached it in a branch made apart from the removal, or after the removal was made (by a change's own time less ten minutes), whichever device carried it, and nothing stored over either: those go from its folders to its local history. A key that the removed device added after its removal is removed too (the decision record of 2026-10-24, rules 2, 6 and 7, and section 8)."

## 9. What it costs

- **A relay holds two change entries of a person's while two changes made apart are both heads,** and keeps room for a second where it has the room: 32 KB more for each person at each relay. A relay that is full at a person's first change made on this version refuses a second head there for room, until it has room, and the status says so (rule 15).
- **A stranger's entries that end with the mark** are kept as heads too, up to eight a slot, within the caps of the channel, the relay and the stranger's allowances (rule 15).
- **A relay reads a change entry's lineage.** It learns how a person's statements follow each other, when two were made apart, and when each was made. It learned before each time a phrase is used.
- **A device of the version before cannot make a change over one made on this version** (section 6).
- **While two heads stand, each new connection to a relay is handed each head once,** 32 KB each. At a relay of the version before, a head set aside that a device shows costs 32 KB each way, once a connection (rule 18).
- **A relay that stalls each show holds a wake for longer.** A round has room for 23 shows, each up to about 30 seconds where the relay holds back each stage of its stream, so one such relay holds the pass of a wake for up to about 11 minutes, where 6 shows held it for about 3. It costs time only: the other relays' shows run alongside it, the wake's own wait (`WAKE_WAIT_SECS`) still ends the wake, and later passes go on at the other relays. No deadline is set on a round, which would be one more timeout (the record of 2026-10-04, 16).
- **A change made apart from a removal is set aside:** a device that it listed, and that the removal's change does not, is in no list, and stops until it is added again or the two are joined. That is so at any change today.
- **Two devices that each follow a clear change made apart go on apart,** and do not sync with each other until a change joins them (rule 11).
- **A closed device stops, as a device in a fork does today,** in fewer cases: only where each change that lists it lists a key removed on another.
- **A move to a lower number** drops a record whose revision is in a band above it, and its file meets the channel as a new file does (rule 9).
- **Shared channels, when built:** every removal makes every shared channel anew; the other people move with a yes each; until each moves or says to stay, their devices send nothing in the old channel, and a member can so pause another person's sending by making a channel anew that leaves out a key.
- **A device's word that it left is not written, or sent, by a device that is closed** (rules 4 and 5).
- **A key that the removed device added after its removal is removed,** with what it wrote; and so is a device that the removed device added honestly in a branch made apart from its removal (rule 6).
- **Each device keeps the time of each change it has applied or kept, and up to 1,024 arrivals a file** (rules 2 and 7).
- **What rule 7 cuts is gone from the folders** of the device that took it, and is in that device's local history alone: where local history is off, it is gone. That is what of the removed device's reached the device in a branch made apart from its removal, or after its cut time, and every version of that file stored after it, a remaining device's own included. A device that did not see the change that first removed the key cuts from an earlier change; one whose generation and every change since were made on the version before cuts all of the key's there. Each sets aside honest writing to stay safe. A change made apart before the removal can hold honest work of the removed device, and that goes too. The status says how many edits were set aside, and `cordelia restore` puts one back.

## 10. Set aside, and why

- **A relay that keeps a few change entries by number alone, with no lineage.** No change to the change entry is needed. But a removed device holds every change of its branch, and by showing them again pushes a removal out of what a relay keeps, wherever its branch has made enough changes since the two parted. That is a failure mode a thief can use.
- **A change entry whose number is ordered by what it removes.** One entry would still be enough where a removal meets a renewal. It cannot order two removals of different devices, which must both reach every device; and devices of the version before refuse an entry whose revision is not its number.
- **A device that writes the other change into the personal channel of a branch that is not clear,** so that devices on that branch learn of it through any relay. It needs an exception to rule 4, and a removed device that holds that channel can fill it so that the entry finds no room. Relays that keep every head do it without either.
- **Every device moves to one clear change by rank,** so that two clear branches join without a change. It moves devices that are safe where they are, and carries everything twice, for something that is not safety.
- **A device that sends only while every relay it is connected to has given it leave in the last 10 seconds,** and other ways to make the window shorter (section 8). One relay would hold up the others, or every stream would gain a stop, for some 20 seconds of a window that has no bound for a device that is off.
- **Making room at a full relay by dropping its newest channels** (the first form of rule 15). A stranger could drive it.
- **Showing a relay every head kept** (the first form of rule 18). A relay of the version before takes the higher in place of a removal.
- **A relay that is told whether it is of this version, by the protocol's version in the handshake,** so that a device can say which relays keep every head. Today the end that proposes offers only its own version (`handshake.rs` 115 and 116), and the end that accepts refuses a proposal that does not include its own (224): raising it needs both changed first, or the two versions refuse each other. A role in the handshake does the same with no such change (rule 18).
- **Carrying each removed key's first-removal time in the lineage** (the second read's first fix for its point 2). It fits, with hashes of 7 bytes. But a maker can only carry a time it has, and a removal it took from a change of the version before has none: that gap stays. Rule 2's cut time uses only times this device has checked, needs no change of format, and is never later than the removal, but for clocks. Its cost is that a device that missed a change sets more aside.
- **Cutting a version whose first link a removed key signed** (the second read's cheap fix for its point 4). A carry of a carry puts that key's link second, and it is missed. Arrivals catch any number of carries, and replace the cut by descent (rule 7).
- **Only the device that makes the removal carries what the removed device wrote** (the stricter way of the record of 2026-10-04, 14, item 1). The person chose the cut by time instead on 25 October (rule 7): no wait for the maker, and no conflict files on every device at every removal.
- **Hiding later statements from a removed device.** A removed device keeps the statement key, and reads each later statement: who the devices are, by key and label, and which made the change (the record of 2026-10-04, 4.6 and section 12). **Decided by the person on 25 October: not in R1, and a known limit** (property 1). If it is done later, the removed keys stay readable to every device that follows the phrase. A device that a branch does not list learns that branch's removals only by reading them: in case 3's first variant, the phone follows the laptop's change 3, which lists the tablet, and is in no list of the desktop's change 3; it closes because it can read that the tablet was removed. So a later format can seal the devices, their labels and the maker to the devices listed, since a device could judge a change by its lineage, and not the removed keys.

## 14. The build, in slices

Each slice is a pull request of its own into the integration branch `feat/removal`, from a branch `feat/removal-<n>`, reviewed in one sitting and tested alone. They come in the order their dependencies need, and **the first is what makes a device fail closed:** from it on, no device seals under a secret it knows a removed key to hold, and none takes what a key it knows to be removed signs. Every later slice only adds what a device comes to know, and how it goes on; none makes a device less safe than the slice before it. Gate 4 of release R1 stays open until slice 8 is merged. A mutation check runs with each slice (section 7): each rule it builds is undone in turn, and the tests that should catch it are run.

Main is now at step 20 of the schema (a person's hold on messages). Each slice that needs a step takes the next: 21, 22, 23 and 24 below.

**Slice 1. A device fails closed: what it knows of removals, and the two gates.** Rules 2, 3 and 4, rule 5 but its reset of a push already open and its wake, and rule 6 but the cut time.

- **Builds.** The removed keys (rule 2), from every change a device sees and checks: the one it applies, one made apart, one behind, one that stops it. They only grow, and are forgotten only where the device leaves its phrase. At most `MAX_KNOWN_REMOVED`; past it the device is closed. The holders of the change followed (rule 3): the keys that records seen under it add, kept apart from the bound of 256, at most `MAX_KNOWN_HOLDERS`; past it the change is not clear. One function says whether a change is clear. `Standing::to_write` and `come_to` refuse a secret that is not clear (rule 4); `applied_secret` refuses nothing. **Closed is worked out, not stored, in this slice:** a device that stands applied under a change that is not clear stands closed (`at_relays::stands`). So from the commit that keeps the key or the record (rule 5's moment), it has no leave, sends nothing (its channels and pair channels are none), takes nothing in a channel of its own at the door, shows each relay its entry, refuses `msg` and `add-device`, keeps new content in its folders, writes no word that it left, and says so in red. The check once a stream is open asks where it stands, and not only the kept entry's ID. A change shown is not applied where it is not clear, and its removed keys are kept; `cordelia accept` refuses a hand-over whose change is not clear. Who counts reads the removed keys (rule 6): the door, records of additions, `add-device`, and the messages a device shows. The carry still counts as today: rule 7 is slice 8.
- **The way on, in this slice,** is a change made with the phrase on the closed device (`cordelia renew`), which lists no removed key; the move of rule 9 is slice 5.
- **Files.** `protocol.rs` (`MAX_CHANGE_HEADS`, to derive from; `MAX_KNOWN_REMOVED`; `MAX_KNOWN_HOLDERS`), `parameter-rationale.md` §12.6; `cordelia-storage`: `schema.rs` (step 21: the removed keys, the holders, and the changes past the bound of holders), `person.rs`; `cordelia-api`: `person.rs`, `publish.rs`, `at_relays.rs`, `take.rs`, `adding.rs`, `leaving.rs`, `look.rs`, `sync.rs`, `messages.rs`, `state.rs`; `cordelia-node`: `device_entries.rs`, `device_entries/leave.rs`, `indicator.rs`; `cordelia-sync`: `claude.rs` (its words).

- **Mutations to catch.** Each gate taken out (`to_write`, `come_to`); the holders kept with the records, so the bound drops them; the bound of holders not failing closed; who counts reading the statement's removed keys alone; `stands` not asking whether the change is clear; the door taking while closed; the removed keys of a change made apart not kept; the check once a stream is open asking the ID alone; the word that it left written while closed.

A recovery's look reads the generation it recovers from through the door as a reader that seals nothing (`take::take_to_read`): it is not refused where that generation is not clear, since what it reads there is what it shows. The removed keys go when a device leaves its phrase, and only then: under another phrase they are another person's keys.
- **Built so, after its review.**
  - **Clear is worked out once for each write that can change it.** The door asks it of each entry, `Leave::has` of each request and `keeps` of each stream, under the node's lock. So step 21 keeps a mark of 16 random bytes (`person_clear_mark`) that a trigger makes anew at every write of what clear is worked out from: the person's row, its records, the removed keys, the holders, the changes past the bound of holders, and the mark past the bound of removed keys. A device works it out once under a mark and reads the mark alone after that. A write undone puts back its mark with what it undid, and a mark is never made twice, so no "clear" is read under a mark that it is not true of; and a trigger fires whatever code writes. It fails closed.
  - **The removed keys of a change refused after its signature is checked are kept:** one shown that undoes a removal, and a hand-over refused at `cordelia accept`, in a transaction of their own, since accept's own is undone whole where its change is not clear.

**Slice 4. Changes made from here** (rules 20, 21, 22). The node hands a command the removed keys, and refuses a change of its own that lacks one or lists one as a device; a change made where a head is set aside joins it, and `Statement::settle` takes more than two heads; a settlement holds the secrets of each branch, the newest first. - **Built so, and why.**
  - **A head on another's chain is joined.** A device sets aside a change made after the one it follows where that change is not clear (rule 8, step 6), so a change made there has on its chain a head that has the change followed on its own. `Statement::settle` refuses only one head, or a head given twice: two of which one is on the other's chain make the statement that the later's next would be, with every removal of each. Refusing them, as the version before did, would leave such a device with no change it can make.
  - **The secret of each branch first.** The part for the phrase holds the secret of every head joined before any secret that a head copies forward, and then the newest of those, as many as fit: a long branch would otherwise push out the secret of a short one.
  - **The node refuses a change of its own** where it does not list every key the device knows to be removed, lists one as a device, or does not have every head set aside on its chain; `cordelia remove-device` and `cordelia renew` there make a settlement, and the change is made over the heads the prompt named, or not at all. **Slice 5. A move** (rule 9). A device whose change is not clear moves, with no phrase, to the best clear change that counts it, at a higher number, the same, or a lower one; a record in a band above it is dropped; a command that waits ends with status 3 and says why. Mutations: no move (closed instead); the move to a lower number refused.

- **Built so, and why.**
  - **Only the move waits for the cycle's turn** (rule 5). Where a relay's answer is judged, the move is held back: the transaction that keeps the head and its removed keys commits at once with the device closed, and the node then moves it as a change of settings, which waits for the sync cycle. Moving in that first transaction would have it undone and done again after the wait, with the device standing on a change that is not clear, and not closed, meanwhile.
  - **A device closed by what it knows growing elsewhere moves at its next pass:** a record at the door that hands a removed key the secret (rule 3), and the keys of a hand-over refused at `cordelia accept`, each close in their own transaction, and the pass moves the device where a head it keeps is clear and lists it. A device closed at its first start moves there (rule 12).
  - **What a move is to, and what it carries.** A head set aside that lists the device, is clear, and whose secret opens for it; and, at `cordelia accept`, the change handed with a record of the device's addition, which counts it by that record alone. The renumbering runs forward only: at a move to the same number or a lower one a revision is not lifted, and one whose band is above the change moved to is not carried, with its folder's record dropped.
  - **A command that waits knows the change it made by its entry** (`cordelia change-made <n> --entry <id>`), so that a move at the same number ends the wait too. The node notes each move with the words the command says; where the removed key holds the secret by a record and is in no list, they say "to which this change hands its secret" in place of "which this change lists". The key is looked for among the records under the change left, and then among its holders, where the bound of 256 dropped its record.
  - **A change left keeps what made it not clear** (rules 3 and 9). At a move the key of each record under the change left is kept as its holder before the records go, and the holders and the mark past their bound of a change go only when it is neither followed nor kept as a head. A move that accepting it makes then waits for the cycle with the device closed.

- **Built so, and why.**
  - **Step 23** makes `entries` again with a column `head` in its key: empty for the row kept by the store's rule (every row a device keeps, and every row at the step), and the entry's ID for a head that a relay keeps beside it. A row stored by the store's rule before the step whose entry carries a lineage (one a relay of the version before took from a device of this version) is a head all the same, and is given its ID at the next write in its slot.

- **Built so, and why.**
  - **The version a file goes back to is kept with its arrivals.** The store keeps only each author's newest entry in a slot, so "the newest version stored before" an arrival is often gone by the carry.   - **"Any chain of the file this device stored before"** is read as the chains of every version the slot holds when the entry arrives, the device's own among them, and the most times each link stood in an arrival kept. A chain that is full (100 links) keeps, with its event, the other keys its links name: a removed key among them is cut all the same.
  - **Past `MAX_FILE_ARRIVALS`** the oldest events go whole, and the newest time of what went is kept for each generation; at a carry it is judged as an arrival of every removed key at that time, and where it is cut the file goes back to before the oldest kept.
  - **Rule 6 spares a key that a change on the chain of the removal lists.** Rule 2 cuts from an earlier change where a device did not see the first removal; with rule 6 that removed honest devices that the removed key added before its removal, a device judging its own addition among them, though changes signed with the phrase list them. Rule 6's intent is the keys that hold the secret by the removed key's record alone: a key that the removal, or a change on its chain, lists as a device is the person's by the phrase, and stays. One that only a change made apart lists is removed, as the fault needs (test 7 (j)).
  - **Rule 6 spares a key that the person, at a recovery's terminal, said they still have.** A recovery that marks the laptop lost leaves out the desktop that the laptop added, and the person adds it again (the record of 2026-10-04, section 9, step 6). Where the laptop added it within ten minutes before the recovery, rule 6 read alone would remove the desktop, and no change brings a removed key back. The person was shown the desktop with the phrase and answered for it, so the device that made the recovery keeps that answer (`person_removed_keys_spared`, step 24) and rule 6 spares that key. A key the person was not asked about, as a removed key's addition made after its removal is, is not spared. A key left out by a change made elsewhere is not spared either, since a removed key's late addition is left out in the same way. On the device itself, its own key is spared as well. Rule 6 guards against keys that the removed device holds by its record alone, and a device knows it holds its own key. The other devices judge that key by what each of them took.
